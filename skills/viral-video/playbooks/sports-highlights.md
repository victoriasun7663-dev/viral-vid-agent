# Playbook: sports and action highlights

From a tennis UGC account: long tripod recordings of practice rallies turned into clips and a highlight reel. The account grew from zero to hundreds of thousands of views in its first weeks.

## Finding the moments

- Detect events from the audio (ball strikes, landings, crowd peaks). Group strikes into rallies; rally length (shot count) is the first ranking.
- Check frames from every candidate. Drop clips where someone walks in front of the camera or the subject isn't in frame.
- Sort into **best** (subject close to camera; the account's usual look) and **extra** (subject far away, still a real rally).
- Name files so they explain themselves: `01_IMG5443_09m08s_21shots.mp4` = rank, source, start time, estimated shots.
- Split anything over 30 s into parts. Most clips land at 10-30 s.
- Long rallies stand in for good points, but miss short points with a great winner. Say so, and offer to cut any moment the user remembers.

## Highlight reel

- 30-45 s. About 7 moments of 5-6 s each, each taken from the stretch of its clip with the most strikes.
- Alternate conditions (day / night, outfits, angles) so it doesn't feel repetitive.
- Hard cuts with a very short audio fade at each cut so there are no pops.
- Keep the natural court sound so the user can add trending music in the app and turn the original down.
- No captions unless asked; creators often add text in the app.

## Account content ideas

- Personality hooks over pure skill: a joke about a pro player, a "POV" about a common club-player moment.
- The growth story itself is content ("fresh account, 20 days, 445K views"), for LinkedIn and X as well as the platform.

## Posting checklist

1. Keep the original frame size (often 720x1280 from phones); don't upscale.
2. Add music and text in the app.
3. Cover frame mid-swing.
